# P7.1 — the Claude Design handoff

**What P7.2 is being asked to design, and what it must not have to rediscover.** The semantics
are settled in the [P7.1 UX contract](p7.1-judge-ux-contract.md); this page is the buildable
brief that follows from them. Read that page first — in particular §4's state table and §9's
anti-patterns, which are constraints rather than suggestions.

**Nothing here is implemented.** No frontend file was changed by this slice. P7.3 implements the
result of the P7.2 pass; this page is the input to that pass and the acceptance list for it.

## What is already locked, and what is genuinely open

| | |
|---|---|
| **Locked — do not redesign** | The five bands and their order. The state vocabulary and every phrase. Which surfaces exist. Which verb each conversation phase permits. What may never be drawn. That the screen renders and does not decide. |
| **Open — design decides** | Everything visual: layout, palette, type, density, rhythm, component form, motion, and how the conversation panel and the bands share a frame. |

The current frontend uses Tailwind v4 with five semantic tokens — `surface`, `panel`, `edge`,
`ink`, `muted` — in oklch ([index.css](../apps/frontend/src/index.css)). **That is the starting
point, not a lock.** The palette and typography may be replaced wholesale. Two things carry
forward regardless: a visible focus ring on everything, and the rule that no state is carried by
colour alone.

## 1. Screens to design

Four surfaces, from §2 of the contract. Only the first two need real design work.

### A — the case workspace (primary)

`/` resolves to the newest case; `/?case=<id>` to a named one. Five bands in fixed order, plus
the conversation panel. This is the screen a judge lands on and the screen the demo is recorded
on; it deserves nearly all the design budget.

Band order, and what each must do visually:

| band | title as shipped | the visual job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What happened | A human being said something. The verbatim quote is the largest piece of text on the screen after the headline. An open question here outranks everything below it. |
| 2 | What you must do now | Exactly one action and exactly one owner. It must be impossible to mistake whose move it is. |
| 3 | What changes, under whose authority | Three groups, always in the same order, always with their titles, even when a group is empty. This is the propagation, and it is the centre of gravity. |
| 4 | What was left alone | Quiet, never hidden, never collapsed by default, always counted. This carries the product's central claim and must be legible in the same glance as band 3. |
| 5 | Evidence | Collapsed. One control. Arrives with the case, so opening costs nothing. |

**Bands 3 and 4 must be readable together.** A judge who has to scroll from the affected set to
the untouched set loses the comparison that is the entire differentiator. On a laptop viewport
this is the single hardest layout constraint in the brief, and it outranks any other composition
preference.

### B — the conversation panel (primary, inside A)

Not a route. A panel that shares the frame with the bands so a spoken turn and its consequence are
one observation. It carries the nine voice states from §7 of the contract and works identically as
text. The transcript is always shown before the turn is taken.

### C — the order book (secondary)

The accepted promises as they stand, plus the case index. Its job in the demo is one beat: the
external order change crossing before anything else happens. Ingredient and equipment figures are
one collapsed section here. Light design work; it must not read as a dashboard.

### D — sign-in (gate)

One action to a real session. A judge must never face a credential field the page does not
satisfy. Minimal design work, maximum clarity about what to press.

## 2. Components

Twelve, and the list is deliberately short. Anything not here should be questioned before it is
drawn.

| component | contract it must honour |
|---|---|
| **Verbatim quote** | Never tidied, truncated with no affordance, or sentence-cased. Attribution and timestamp attached. |
| **Open question** | Question, options from the delivery's own rows, free-text answer. Focus of the screen while open. Never a toast or a dismissible modal. |
| **Next-action banner** | One action, one owner label, always populated — `NOBODY` is a real answer and reads as one. |
| **Authority group header** | The backend's title, shown even when the group is empty. |
| **Promise row** | Customer, external order id, **phrase**, **state name as text**, reason, deadline, next action. Phrase and state name both, always. |
| **State badge** | Text first, tone second. See §5 below on the current tone function. |
| **Causal chain** | Fixed columns, identical geometry per row, threatened promises only. Needs a P7.3 backend field. |
| **Untouched row** | Quiet register, reason attached, never a success treatment. |
| **Count** | Renders a backend number. Has no arithmetic in it. |
| **Evidence drawer** | Collapsed, one toggle, monospace inside, `expected` beside `actual`. |
| **Conversation turn** | Worker turn and system turn visually distinct; the system's words are the backend's, unedited. |
| **Voice control** | Push-to-talk, large, keyboard-operable, with a visible state at all times. |

No component in this list computes a value. If one needs to, the field is missing and §3 of the
contract's evidence table is where to say so.

## 3. Hierarchy

One reading order, and it is the same as the band order. Weighted roughly:

1. **The headline and the quote.** What happened, in a person's words.
2. **The one next action.** Whose move it is.
3. **The three authority groups.** Who decides each change.
4. **The untouched set with its count.** What this did not touch.
5. **Everything else**, including the evidence control, the order-book link and the case index.

The conversation panel is a persistent peer of 1–4 rather than a sixth item: it is where the case
moves, so it is always present and never competes for the top of the reading order.

## 4. Interaction and motion

Motion has exactly one job here: **make a state change noticeable after it has rendered.** It
never gates a read and never runs before content is legible.

| moment | what may move | what may not |
|---|---|---|
| A promise changes state | the row's state badge and phrase transition in place | the row reordering, the group re-sorting, or a celebratory flourish |
| A promise moves between authority groups | the move is shown, and the new group is briefly marked | the layout reflowing so a judge loses their place |
| The plan is confirmed | the confirm affordance leaves; band 3 re-renders from the backend | anything optimistic, anticipatory, or applied locally |
| An effect completes | the evidence drawer's row gains its provider reference | a success animation on the band-3 row before the state says `RECOVERED` |
| The drawer opens | it opens | it fetches |
| A voice turn is captured | the transcript appears | the turn being taken before the transcript is visible |

Every interactive element is keyboard-reachable and keyboard-operable, and the whole judge journey
from §1 of the contract is completable with a keyboard alone.

## 5. Two concrete things to fix while designing

Both are in the current implementation and both are visual decisions, so they belong to this pass
rather than to P7.3's mechanics.

1. **`toneForState` decides by substring.** It matches on fragments of a state name
   ([tones.ts](../apps/frontend/src/components/tones.ts)) — which means the screen is deriving a
   meaning rather than being given one. Replace it with an explicit mapping over the fourteen
   promise states in §4 of the contract, so a new state is a mapping error rather than a silent
   neutral badge. Note in passing that its `UNAFFECTED → good` rule would render "left alone" as a
   success if it were ever reached by a promise state; the explicit table removes the risk.
2. **The empty states are engineering-console copy.** "Run `uv run pp reset-demo-state`" and
   "check that the backend is running" are instructions to a developer. Every empty and error
   state needs a sentence a baker would say — the case-read failure copy in
   [CaseWorkspace.tsx](../apps/frontend/src/features/case/CaseWorkspace.tsx) is already the right
   register and is the model for the rest.

## 6. Responsive behaviour

| viewport | what must hold |
|---|---|
| **Laptop, 1280–1600** | The primary target — it is what the demo is recorded on. Bands 3 and 4 readable together. Conversation panel visible without scrolling. |
| **Large desktop** | Extra width goes to the conversation panel and the causal chain, never to a third column of new content. |
| **Tablet** | The conversation panel may become a sheet over the bands. Band order never changes. |
| **Phone** | Single column, band order preserved, the conversation panel reachable in one action. The untouched band is **never** the thing that gets dropped to save space. |

A bakery worker is standing up with their hands busy, so the phone layout is a real use case and
not a courtesy. Voice controls are large and reachable one-handed.

## 7. Demo-critical moments

Five frames the recording depends on. Each must be composable in a single unscrolled viewport,
because each is a shot.

1. **The order book before anything happens** — the accepted promises, with Lena's external change
   crossing into it.
2. **The open question** — the case has concluded nothing and the screen visibly says so. This is
   the frame that proves ambiguity stops mutation.
3. **`PLANNED`, in full** — three authority groups populated, the untouched set beside them, and
   **not one completion mark anywhere on the screen.** The most important frame in the submission.
4. **Mid-recovery** — `EXT-A` reading "changing the order now" while `EXT-B` reads "asked" and
   `EXT-C` reads "needs you". Three different truths at once, in one glance.
5. **Settled** — the closing summary rendered from real state, with the untouched count and the
   zero incident-caused effects beside it.

Frame 3 is the one a judge should still remember afterwards. Design it first.

## 8. Accessibility

Not a compliance pass at the end — several of these are the same rules truthfulness already
imposes.

- **No state carried by colour alone.** Already required by the contract; it also makes every
  frame survive a monochrome video.
- **Contrast** at WCAG AA or better for body text, and for the quiet untouched band too. Quiet
  means lower emphasis, not lower legibility.
- **Keyboard-complete.** The whole judge journey and the whole worker journey, including the
  conversation panel and the drawer, with a visible focus ring throughout — the global
  `:focus-visible` rule in [index.css](../apps/frontend/src/index.css) stays.
- **Screen-reader order matches visual order**, band by band. The drawer is a proper disclosure
  with `aria-expanded`, as it already is.
- **Live regions used sparingly and honestly.** A promise changing state is worth announcing; a
  freshness timestamp ticking is not.
- **Large controls**, especially push-to-talk, for hands-busy one-handed use.
- **Text equivalent for every voice interaction.** Voice is a path, never the only path.
- **No motion requirement.** Everything is readable and operable with `prefers-reduced-motion`
  honoured, which follows anyway from motion never gating a read.

## 9. Acceptance — what P7.2 hands back

A pass is complete when all of these are true.

- [ ] Every screen in §1 designed, at every viewport in §6.
- [ ] All fourteen promise states and all nine case headlines have a specified treatment, and the
      forbidden readings in §4 of the contract are impossible to produce.
- [ ] The five demo-critical frames in §7 each compose in one unscrolled laptop viewport.
- [ ] Bands 3 and 4 are readable together at 1280 wide.
- [ ] No number, sentence, count or state name in any mock is invented — every one traces to a
      field in §6 of the contract, or is marked as a P7.3 backend addition.
- [ ] Nothing from §9 of the contract appears anywhere in the pass.
- [ ] The accessibility list in §8 is satisfied in the design, not deferred to implementation.
