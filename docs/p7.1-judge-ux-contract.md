# P7.1 — the judge-facing UX contract

**Locked before any visual work, because P7 is a design-led phase.** This page fixes what the
product's surfaces are, what a judge must understand and in what order, what each truthful state
is allowed to look like, and what may never be drawn. It is the input to the P7.2 visual pass and
to the P7.3 implementation that follows it.

**Nothing here is implemented.** No frontend was changed, no AWS resource was touched and nothing
was deployed by this slice. Where this page names a value the screen will show, it also names the
field it comes from — or says plainly that no such field exists yet and that P7.3 must add it. A
requirement whose data does not exist is a requirement to build the data, never a licence to
compose the value on a screen.

Nothing here redesigns frozen architecture or reopens a closed gate. The state vocabulary, the
five workspace bands and the zero-effect claim are the [P5 product
contract](p5-product-contract.md)'s and are quoted rather than restated. The canonical outcomes
are [P5.4](p5.4-truthful-recovery-and-case-workspace.md)'s. The scenario labels are the [frozen
manifest](effect-set-manifest.md)'s, content hash
`d41f5afcd01eda8e6fa4c28784f1fb0c238bbc27711019aac670914db62b2cdc`.

## The one sentence the whole surface exists to produce

> One missing delivery. PromisePatch finds which cakes are at risk, coordinates the permitted
> fixes, and leaves the other orders alone.

A judge who can say that back after a minute has understood the product. A judge who instead says
"it is a bakery stock dashboard" has seen a different product than the one that was built, and the
screen is what made the difference. Every decision below is answerable to that sentence.

## 1. The judge journey

### Where a judge actually lands today

`https://184.194.40.87.sslip.io/` currently answers with one line of plain text and a `404`
([Caddyfile](../deploy/compose/Caddyfile)) — the evidence UI is not deployed. So the journey below
is authored from zero rather than from an existing page that needs improving.

### The rule the entry has to satisfy

**One deliberate action, no documentation, no CLI, and a real case at the end of it.** A judge who
must read a README to find a route, run a command to seed a case, or hunt through navigation has
already spent the minute this contract is budgeting. The roadmap's phrasing for G7 is *"job
completed without CLI"*; the judge's equivalent is *understanding reached without instructions*.

**Open question for P7.3, not decided here.** A judge arrives with no session, and every case read
runs the domain's own `require_permitted` check. Two mechanisms satisfy the rule: a published
read-only judge account whose credentials are on the sign-in screen itself, or a one-click judge
session issued by the API under the same permission checks. The recommended default is the second,
because it costs the judge no typing. Either way the worker session is not weakened, no check is
bypassed, and the judge's session is subject to the same domain permission the MCP surface uses. A
sign-in screen that asks for credentials it does not supply is forbidden: it makes the entire
submission invisible.

### The five beats, with their budgets

Times are comprehension budgets for the design to hit, not measured numbers. Nothing has been
timed.

| beat | by | surface | what must be legible without help |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Arrival | 0:05 | `/` | One line of framing, then a **real case already open**. Not a hero page, not a marketing block, not a list, not an empty dashboard. |
| 1. Somebody said something | 0:15 | band 1 | A worker's own sentence, in quotation marks, verbatim, with who said it and when — and the clarifying question that sentence needed, with the answer that was given. This is the spoken exception made concrete. |
| 2. It reached different promises differently | 0:30 | band 3 | Six customer orders resolved into **three authority groups** — covered by a standing preference / needs the customer / needs the owner — each row carrying its customer, its state in words, and the one-line reason it landed in that group. |
| 3. And it left the rest alone | 0:45 | band 4 | The untouched orders, **counted against the case's own universe**, each with the reason it is untouched, beside the case's zero incident-caused operational effects. |
| 4. None of this is staged | 1:00 | evidence drawer | One click opens identifiers, provider references, the revalidation checks with the values they compared, and the external order system's own version. The drawer proves the four bands above were read rather than written. |

Beats 2 and 3 together are the whole differentiator: **the same physical fact producing different
consequences under different authorities, and no consequence at all where there is no path.** If a
judge remembers one screen, it is the one where those two bands are visible at once.

### What the journey deliberately does not do in the first minute

- It does not start in the conversation. Watching a conversation happen is the second minute and
  the demo video's job; a judge who lands mid-turn sees a chat box, not a product.
- It does not start in the order book. Six rows with no exception on them is a list of cakes.
- It does not ask the judge to choose a case. There is a newest one; open it.
- It does not animate the propagation before the outcome is readable. Motion that has to finish
  before the screen can be read costs the beat it was meant to sell.

## 2. Information architecture

### The frozen surface list

**Four surfaces. Two of them primary, and the second of those lives inside the first.** The
alternative — cases, conversation, promises, resources, evidence and status as six peers behind a
navigation bar — is the "four engineering dashboards" the roadmap names as a rejected vanity
outcome, and it buries selective propagation under navigation.

| # | surface | reached by | its one job | status today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Case workspace** | `/` (newest case) and `/?case=<id>` | The operational surface. The five bands of one real case. | exists, minimal — [CaseWorkspace.tsx](../apps/frontend/src/features/case/CaseWorkspace.tsx) |
| **B** | **Conversation** | a panel **inside A**, never its own route | Report, answer the question, review and confirm — by voice or text, through the same MCP tools. | not built; P7 |
| **C** | **Order book** | a secondary view from A | The accepted promises as they stand, and the index of cases. Context, not a dashboard. | exists as Live Operations — [LiveOperations.tsx](../apps/frontend/src/features/live-ops/LiveOperations.tsx) |
| **D** | **Sign-in** | `/` while signed out | One action to a real session. | exists — [LoginScreen.tsx](../apps/frontend/src/features/auth/LoginScreen.tsx) |

Nothing else is a surface. The evidence drawer is band 5 of A. The dependency view is part of band
3 of A. Ingredient and equipment figures are evidence, and are demoted to one collapsed section of
C rather than deleted — they are real reads that back the revalidation checks, and they are not a
screen a worker starts the day on.

### Why the conversation is a panel and not a screen

G7 says each concept need not become a separate screen, and this is the concept where that matters
most. **A worker speaks and the case changes; those are one event and belong in one frame.** Split
across two routes, a judge sees a chat box on one screen and an outcome on another, and has to
take the causal link on trust. Side by side, the link is the observation. This is the
demo-critical moment of the whole product, and it is a decision about what the screen *is*, not a
layout preference.

The panel creates no authority. It calls the same four tools over the same authenticated transport
the `converse` client uses; the case still moves only through the domain; no button in the
workspace changes a case, and none is added by this contract.

### Routing and durability

The case id lives in the address bar and nothing else holds it — already true
([App.tsx](../apps/frontend/src/app/App.tsx), `useCaseRoute`). A reload, a restored tab, a second
browser and a restarted worker all land on the same durable case in the same state, and the screen
must never suggest otherwise. `/` with no `case` parameter resolves to the newest case the worker
may open; `/` with no case in existence is an honest empty state that says how a case is opened,
never an instruction to run a command.

### What is explicitly not in the architecture

No global navigation bar. No settings screen. No notification centre. No search. No filters. No
per-user preferences. No second vertical. No order editor — PromisePatch has no order editor, and
the external system is the record. Each of these is a page a judge could open to find nothing the
product claims.

## 3. The canonical Hollow Oak demo

### Which scenario the demo is

**`S11`, `external-edit-removes-dependency-before`** — the frozen manifest's own demo order of
events, and the one the roadmap's storyboard follows. `S01` is the same incident *without* the
external edit, and `S02` is the whole-delivery counterfactual. All three are hand-labelled in the
frozen manifest and none of their labels may be edited to suit a screen.

The demo is `S11` rather than `S01` for one reason, and the design must make that reason visible:
**Lena's own change to her own order removes her raspberry dependency before the incident ever
happens.** She is untouched because of what is stored, not because somebody arranged for her to
be. That is what makes the outcome contingent on current reality rather than on a prepared table,
and it is the difference between a demo and a screenshot.

### The six promises, as `S11` labels them

Customers and orders are the fixture's (`promise_graph.examples.hollow_oak`); classifications and
endings are [P5.4](p5.4-truthful-recovery-and-case-workspace.md)'s proven outcomes.

| order | customer | classification | authority band | ending | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EXT-A` | Priya Nair | `AUTO_RECOVERABLE` | Covered by a standing preference | **RECOVERED** — "changed" | a pre-authored strawberry version exists and her recorded preapproval already permits it |
| `EXT-B` | Tomas Lindqvist | `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` | Needs the customer | **REQUESTED** — "asked" | a pre-authored version exists, but his recorded constraint is to be asked before a visible change |
| `EXT-C` | Okafor-Reyes | `BLOCKED` | Needs the owner | **ESCALATED** — "needs you" | the order forbids substitution and no variant of the Charlotte is authored — two independent reasons |
| `EXT-D` | Lena Fischer | `UNAFFECTED` | Left alone | **UNTOUCHED** — "left alone" | **her own earlier external edit** moved her order off the raspberry version, so no path reaches her |
| `EXT-E` | Ahmed Bouazizi | `UNAFFECTED` | Left alone | **UNTOUCHED** — "left alone" | the ganache reserves dark chocolate and cream; no path runs from a raspberry shortfall |
| `EXT-F` | Cafe Marlow | `UNAFFECTED` | Left alone | **UNTOUCHED** — "left alone" | the danishes reserve blueberries and butter; no path runs from a raspberry shortfall |

`S11`'s declared untouched baseline is **3**, so the published figure the screen carries is **0
incident-caused operational effects on 3 of 6 orders**. Under `S01` — the same incident with no
prior edit — `EXT-D` is `BLOCKED` instead and the baseline is 2. **The screen hardcodes neither
number.** `untouched_count` and `threatened_count` arrive from the backend for exactly this reason.

### The demo sequence, and which surface carries each step

| # | what happens | surface | what a judge sees change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lena changes `EXT-D` in the external order system; the signed event crosses and the mirror moves to external version 2 | order book (C) | one order changes before anything else happens, in a system that is visibly not ours |
| 2 | Maya says "today's raspberry delivery didn't arrive" | conversation panel (B) | her words appear verbatim in band 1, in quotation marks |
| 3 | The case asks: the whole Valley Produce delivery, or just the raspberries? | band 1 + panel | an **open question**, not a result. Nothing has been decided and the screen says so |
| 4 | Maya answers: just the raspberries — the strawberries came | panel | the question closes with her answer beside it, and the strawberry receipt is attested |
| 5 | Analysis completes; the case reaches `PLANNED` | bands 3 and 4 | six orders resolve into three authority groups and one untouched group — **every threatened promise reads "planned — waiting for you"** |
| 6 | Maya reads the plan and says yes | panel | one explicit confirmation, bound to the exact plan she was read |
| 7 | Recovery runs | band 3 | `EXT-A` goes `AUTHORIZED` → `APPLYING` → `RECOVERED` only once the order system's own event returns; `EXT-B` goes `PLANNED` → `REQUESTED` only once the provider acknowledges delivery; `EXT-C` sits at `ESCALATED` with an owner, a reason and a next action |
| 8 | Tomas replies `YES` from his own channel | band 3 | `EXT-B` goes `CONSENTED`, then `APPLYING`, then `RECOVERED` |
| 9 | Maya asks for status | panel | a sentence naming what changed, what still needs the owner, and that the other three were left alone — **rendered by `status_view`, never composed on the screen** |

Steps 5 and 7 are where the product is won or lost. Between them, **nothing external has happened,
and the screen must say nothing external has happened.**

## 4. The truthful state contract, as screen behaviour

The vocabulary is the [P5 product contract](p5-product-contract.md)'s and is not reopened here.
What is fixed here is what each state is allowed to *look* like. The phrases below are the exact
strings `promisepatch.domain.status_view` renders; **a screen shows the phrase it is given and
never a synonym**, because a paraphrase of "planned" is one word away from "done".

### Every promise state

`owner` is the backend's `ActionOwner` for that state, and it decides which person the row is
addressed to.

| state | phrase, exactly | owner | the screen may | the screen may never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `UNTOUCHED` | left alone | NOBODY | be quiet, low-contrast, grouped, and counted | be omitted, collapsed by default, or drawn as a success |
| `PLANNED` | planned - waiting for you | YOU | show the proposed change and a confirm affordance | show a tick, a completion tone, or any past-tense verb |
| `AUTHORIZED` | covered by a standing preference | SYSTEM | read as permission granted and work queued | read as done, or share a visual with `RECOVERED` |
| `REQUESTED` | asked | CUSTOMER | show the customer's deadline and a waiting posture | imply an answer, agreement, or that the change is made |
| `CONSENTED` | said yes | SYSTEM | show the customer's literal decision and that work is queued | show the change as applied |
| `DECLINED` | said no | OWNER | read as a settled answer the owner takes from here | read as a failure of the system, or as an error state |
| `APPLYING` | changing the order now | SYSTEM | show in-flight work honestly | show a completed change, a tick, or a recovered count |
| `RECOVERED` | changed | NOBODY | be the one state that reads as finished | appear before the external order system's own state confirms it |
| `ESCALATED` | needs you | OWNER | be prominent, with owner, reason and next action | be styled as an error, a crash, or a system fault |
| `STALE` | the plan no longer fits - re-planned | OWNER | explain that revalidation refused and the track was re-planned | be silent, auto-dismissed, or shown as a retry in progress |
| `EXPIRED` | no answer by the deadline | OWNER | state that the timer fired with no decision | be shown as a decline — nobody said no |
| `LINKED` | already being handled by another case | NOBODY | sit outside the untouched band, with its own phrasing | be counted as untouched — it was left to somebody else, not left alone |
| `WITHDRAWN` | withdrawn | NOBODY | sit outside the untouched band | be counted as untouched |
| `AWAITING_PLAN` | not decided yet | NOBODY | read as the case still deciding | read as any outcome at all — this is the fail-closed default |

`LINKED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `AWAITING_PLAN` are not in the P5 contract's eleven-row table but are
real values the projection returns. A design that handles only eleven renders one of them as a
blank row, which is the one thing a fail-closed default exists to prevent.

### Every case headline

`CaseHeadline` is what the case as a whole has reached, and it sets the frame the bands sit in.

| headline | the frame it sets | notes for design |
|---|---|---|
| `UNDERSTANDING` | the report has landed and is being read | no outcome is available; an empty band 3 must not render as "nothing affected" |
| `CLARIFYING` | there is an open question | band 1 carries a **question**, and it is the visual focus of the whole screen |
| `NEEDS_HUMAN` | the reading did not conclude | the worker is asked to say what the report means. A normal posture, not an error |
| `ANALYSED` | analysis finished, no plan on offer yet | band 3 may show groups; nothing is confirmable |
| `PLANNED` | a plan is on offer and **nothing has been done** | the only headline under which a confirm affordance may exist |
| `WORKING` | confirmed, and work is running | progress is honest; no aggregate success is shown |
| `WAITING` | the case is waiting on a customer or a timer | the waiting is the state, and it is displayed as the state |
| `SETTLED` | terminal | the only headline under which a closing summary may be shown |
| `CANCELLED` | the case was cancelled | no outcome claim of any kind |

### The five rules a screen can break that nothing else can

1. **Nothing may imply an external effect before the worker's confirmation.** While the case is
   `PLANNED`, every threatened promise reads "planned — waiting for you", the count of recovered
   orders is zero, and there is no tick anywhere on the screen. A progress bar, a pre-filled
   success tone or an optimistic row is a lie the backend cannot catch, because the backend was
   never asked.
2. **Permission is not an act, and an acknowledgement is not an outcome.** `AUTHORIZED`,
   `APPLYING` and `RECOVERED` are three different things and must be three different readings.
   `REQUESTED` means a provider acknowledged delivery; it does not mean anybody replied.
3. **Worker confirmation is never drawn in the same language as customer consent.** Different
   people, different authorities, recorded separately. A shared "approved" badge would collapse
   the product's central distinction into a colour.
4. **Every count on the screen is the backend's.** `untouched_count` and `threatened_count` are
   carried explicitly so a screen cannot recompute them from a list it might have filtered,
   paginated or deduplicated. No total, percentage or ratio is derived in the frontend.
5. **Status is legible with no colour at all.** Every state shows its phrase *and* its state name
   as text. Tone is decoration on top of that and never the only carrier — which is also what
   makes the screen survive a monochrome video frame.
